Well, we have some idea what the new scoring system looks like now.

It looks like Joel scoring 163.02 against the guy who ranked him seventh.

That guy was me.

I spent the preseason explaining why my roster belonged third, why Joel was just outside the playoffs, and why giving quarterbacks more value was a reasonable idea. Joel responded by beating me by 42.87.

An unnecessarily thorough response.

Elsewhere, Jacob started his attempt at the league's first repeat championship by scoring more than everybody. Deco won by 78.17 despite getting 2.70 from Ja'Marr Chase. Hutch won a game without requiring a congressional investigation into probability. And Sam's leading scorer was his kicker.

Seven years of league history, an entirely new scoring economy, and somehow we still arrived at twelve familiar problems.

## From the Chat: Joel's New America

Matt supplied the opening review of the scoring changes:

> Maye throwing 3 INT and still having 15 points is Joel's new America we're living in

Maye finished with 15.38 fantasy points. Matt finished with a win. His complaint has been entered into the record alongside his acceptance of the benefits.

Alex then admitted he hadn't realized passing touchdowns were worth six. His assessment: "That's excessive imo."

We passed the scoring overhaul 10–1 and apparently distributed the reading material afterward.

I moved the investigation to kicking, asking why missed field goals weren't getting punished before discovering the distance distinction. Then I accused Joel's kicker of benefiting and deployed "HOW CONVENIENT" in all caps.

For the record, the actual settings penalize missed field goals under 50 yards by one point and apply no deduction from 50-plus. Passing touchdowns are worth six; interceptions cost one.

That is the rulebook. My theory about its suspicious convenience remains a losing owner's chat message.

The season was barely underway and we already had a new national economy, an owner discovering the tax code, and me demanding a kicker audit.

## Jacob Has Apparently Decided to Keep the Trophy

**The Dog 181.24, #TrustTheProcess 122.53**

In the preview, I picked Jacob to win the championship. This is the part of the issue where I briefly direct everyone's attention toward something I got right.

Derrick Henry scored 37.72. Jahmyr Gibbs added 35.42. Patrick Mahomes had 27.22. Those three alone produced 100.36, which would have beaten two complete lineups this week.

Jacob also got 19.53 from Nico Collins, 18.05 from Parker Washington and 17 from Jacksonville's defense. George Kittle contributed 2.70 and was apparently excused from the group project.

JP wasn't terrible. Bijan Robinson put up 29.98, Chris Olave had 26.70, and 122.53 was the sixth-highest score in the league.

He just drew the guy holding the trophy and a flamethrower.

Jalen Coker's 32.80 sat outside JP's starting lineup. Starting him over A.J. Brown's 4.85 would have added 27.95. JP still loses by 30.76.

Sometimes the process needs work. Sometimes the schedule hands you Jacob.

## Joel's Scoring Revolution Has Its First Victim

**Amon Ra Doggin 163.02, 3rdDownConversionTherapy 120.15**

I would like to clarify that ranking Joel seventh was an exercise in motivational leadership.

Kenneth Walker scored 35.44. Lamar Jackson added 29.52. Amon-Ra St. Brown had 26.20, and Javonte Williams supplied 22.66. Four starters over 22 points is a pretty effective way to tell the newsletter writer to shut the hell up.

Christian Watson tried to keep my operation respectable with 32.20. Jayden Daniels gave me 22.06 and Bucky Irving added 18.44. Then George Pickens and Jake Ferguson combined for 7.15, which feels less like a supporting cast and more like two people who stopped by.

There were better choices on my bench. None of them explain away a 42.87-point loss.

Also worth filing: Joel acquired Walker from Jordan on August 2 for DK Metcalf, David Montgomery and Joel's 2027 first. Walker has delivered one very good week. Jordan still owns the pick. We are not declaring the entire trade settled after one Sunday.

But Joel is absolutely allowed to enjoy the screenshot.

Deco begins another attempt to return to the championship game. Sam begins collecting material for the week he eventually ruins somebody else's season.

## Hutch Won a Normal Football Game. Allegedly.

Josh Allen scored 41.14. Dallas Goedert supplied 22.70. Tyler Loop added 15.20. Hutch won by 23.71.

After an entire preseason paragraph about last year's close-game sorcery, he appears to have discovered the much simpler strategy of starting Josh Allen when Josh Allen scores 41 points.

Jordan got 29.00 from David Montgomery and 17 from the Raiders' defense. Kyle Pitts gave him zero.

There is an alternate universe where Jordan wins 136.59–136.35. It requires three changes: Jacoby Brissett over Baker, Juwan Johnson over Pitts, and Jacory Croskey-Merritt over DK Metcalf at FLEX.

Three correct hindsight decisions to win by 0.24. Not one obvious button he forgot to press.

Jordan starts 0–1. His offseason commitment to winning more games remains a work in progress.

## Week 2: Somebody Has to Be 0–2

**Speers vs. JP:** Both 0–1. I ranked myself third before the season; he just outscored me. One of us is about to develop a very strong opinion about sample size. **My pick: Speers.** Editorial independence remains under review.

**Jacob vs. Jordan:** The highest-scoring team gets an owner holding Jacob's 2027 first. Jordan can improve his own record and potentially improve that asset with the same result. **My pick: Jacob.**

**Hutch vs. Matt:** The only matchup between two 1–0 teams. Somebody reaches 2–0, and Matt can test whether scheduling efficiency is a sustainable offense. **My pick: Hutch.**

**Deco vs. Kyle:** My preseason playoff flag for Kyle is getting a deeply unhelpful second assignment. Jeanty gives him a reason to show up; Deco's roster gives me a reason to pick the other side. **My pick: Deco.**

**Alex vs. Brandon:** Brandon needs a better second week, and Alex has already demonstrated that the running backs are participating. **My pick: Alex.**

Those are predictions, not projections. Nobody has played Week 2 yet in this snapshot.

## Final Thoughts

One week doesn't settle a dynasty league. It does give everyone something more useful than an August roster screenshot.

Jacob has the trophy and the high score. Deco still has an absurd lineup. Joel has earned an apology that I believe this issue has now provided in sufficient detail.

Matt and Hutch are unbeaten. Kyle and JP have explanations. Brandon has time. Sam has a kicker. Jordan has our draft picks.

And I have the password to the newsletter.
