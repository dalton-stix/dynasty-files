We're back.

The NFL season is almost here, which means the eight-month stretch where every dynasty roster looks good on paper is officially ending.

Every rookie was a steal. Every veteran has "one more good year." Every rebuilding team is suddenly "closer than people think." Every contender got deeper.

And before a single meaningful snap has even been played, Joel has already managed to create the first controversy of the season by apparently deciding our seven-year-old scoring system needed a full economic restructuring.

Quarterbacks needed more value. Different types of running backs needed to be rewarded. Receivers needed balancing. At one point, we were going to play a second game every week against the league median.

Within about 15 minutes, Alex had accidentally calculated that 20 carries for 50 yards would score 21 points, Sam was questioning whether changing positional values seven years into a dynasty league defeated the purpose of dynasty, and I somehow got accused of promoting socialism because I wanted having Josh Allen or Patrick Mahomes to actually matter.

Fair.

The median proposal eventually went down 7-5.

The rest of Joel's scoring revolution survived 10-1 under the official platform of:

<PullQuote>
Fuck it, let's see how it goes for a year.
</PullQuote>

Democracy.

## Three Guys Bought the First Round

The other major offseason development happened during the rookie draft, where apparently most of the league decided first-round picks were optional.

Brandon, JP and Matt owned nine of the twelve first-round selections.

Nine.

Brandon had 1.01, 1.03 and 1.08.

JP had 1.04, 1.05 and 1.07.

Matt had 1.06, 1.09 and 1.11.

Sam managed to squeeze in twice, Hutch appeared once, and the other seven owners watched.

That is an absurd concentration of draft capital.

Brandon came away with Jeremiyah Love, Jordyn Tyson and Omar Cooper, which gives his roster a completely different feel entering this season. He still needs the young pieces to actually become fantasy producers, but this is probably the most interesting Brandon roster we've seen in a while.

JP grabbed Makai Lemon, Jadarian Price and Fernando Mendoza. Three picks in four selections is either the beginning of #TrustTheProcess finally paying dividends or material for an incredible callback three years from now. There is no middle ground.

And Matt took KC Concepcion, Jonathon Brooks and Jonah Coleman, all of whom I assume he is emotionally attached to until approximately Week 4, when one of them gets included in a six-player trade.

Sam took Carnell Tate at 1.02 and Kenyon Sadiq at 1.12, which fits where his roster is right now. He's rebuilding, he knows he's rebuilding, and for once we don't have to pretend a 32-year-old running back is somehow part of a five-year plan.

Then Hutch arrived at 1.10, took Denzel Boston, and went home.

Maybe that's the secret.

Limit Hutch to one decision at a time.

## Preseason Power Rankings

These are not dynasty-value rankings.

I don't care whose roster is going to be worth the most on a trade calculator in 2029.

This is simple:

If we played tomorrow, who do I think is best?

### 1. Drunken Monkeys — Deco

Boring answer. Correct answer.

Jalen Hurts, Ja'Marr Chase, CeeDee Lamb, Justin Jefferson and Trey McBride is stupid.

More importantly, Deco has made three straight championship games. He won in 2023, lost to Speers in 2024 and lost to Jacob last year.

There are only so many times you can call that luck.

The question isn't whether Deco is good enough to get there again.

It's whether a third straight championship loss officially turns this into the Buffalo Bills.

### 2. The Dog — Jacob

The defending champion stays near the top until somebody proves otherwise.

Mahomes. Gibbs. McCaffrey. Nico Collins. Derrick Henry.

Jacob's roster isn't built around accumulating theoretical future value. It's built around scoring a shitload of points before some of his veterans turn into pumpkins.

Good.

He just beat Deco for the title. Now he gets a shot at something nobody in this league has done yet:

Go back-to-back.

### 3. 3rdDownConversionTherapy — Speers

Yes, I'm ranking myself third.

File a complaint.

Jayden Daniels, De'Von Achane, Malik Nabers and Bucky Irving give this team enough youth that it doesn't feel like a one-year window, but enough current production that "we're building for the future" would be an unacceptable excuse.

This team should make the playoffs.

Anything less would be embarrassing, which feels like a dangerous sentence to publish before Week 1.

### 4. Domestic Abusers — Alex

Alex is never the team everybody spends August talking about.

Then December shows up and he's usually still around.

Burrow, Puka and JSN give him enough high-end offense to beat anybody, and Alex has generally been one of the more competent owners in the league.

The only real question is whether he'll decide on his Week 1 FLEX before Week 2.

### 5. Kylelucas28 — Kyle

I have uncomfortable news.

Kyle might actually be good.

Dak Prescott. James Cook. Ashton Jeanty. Jaylen Waddle. Brock Bowers.

That's a real roster.

Not "pretty good considering Kyle drafted Baker Mayfield in the first round of the startup."

Actually good.

Then, almost immediately, the fantasy gods heard me type "Kyle might actually be good" and went to work. Jeanty is dealing with what's being reported as a sprained ankle, not a torn ACL, and Raiders coach Klint Kubiak has called him "on the mend," though there's no firm return timeline yet.

I'm not panicking and I'm not moving Kyle in these rankings. I'm just saying the timing checked out.

Seven years of rehabilitation have led us here.

If Kyle makes a legitimate run this year, one of the league's foundational jokes may be in jeopardy.

Just kidding.

Baker is forever.

### 6. hburns — Hutch

Last season Hutch committed crimes against probability.

He just kept winning close games.

Every week I predicted regression. Every week Hutch would win something like 88.42 to 86.90 and continue living his best life.

Eventually, you have to respect the results.

Josh Allen and Saquon give the roster enough actual firepower that a playoff appearance wouldn't be surprising.

I just need to know whether the dark magic transferred to 2026.

### 7. Amon Ra Doggin — Joel

Putting the commissioner seventh after he personally redesigned the league's scoring system feels dangerous.

Lamar and Amon-Ra give him enough firepower to beat anybody, and Joel spent half the summer explaining why the new scoring system was going to make every position more valuable.

Unfortunately, one of the players who was supposed to benefit from that new economy, Josh Jacobs, won't even be available to start the season. Jacobs was placed on the Commissioner's Exempt List just before Week 1, with no clear timetable yet for his return.

That's a legitimate early hit to a roster I already had sitting on the playoff line.

There is also something very Joel about spending an offseason carefully rebalancing the value of every position in fantasy football only to immediately lose one of your best running backs before the season starts.

And I still refuse to completely rule out Jacob's theory that the entire scoring system was designed so Lamar can go 10-for-10 for 80 yards and four touchdowns and score 47 fantasy points.

Preseason verdict: Dangerous enough to make the playoffs, thin enough that the Jacobs situation matters immediately.

### 8. brandonreeve — Brandon

My preseason wild card.

Herbert. Drake London. Ladd McConkey.

Now add three first-round rookies.

Brandon's roster could look dramatically better six months from now than it did six months ago.

The question is whether the rookies arrive quickly enough to make this a 2026 story or whether we're still a year away.

Either way, there's finally enough young talent here to make Brandon dangerous.

Which is unfortunate because he's already difficult enough to trade with.

### 9. '21 — Matt

Ranking Matt's roster in August feels pointless because there's about a 4% chance this is still Matt's roster in November.

Drake Maye gives him a really interesting young quarterback, and the rookie draft gave him three more assets to play with.

He'll be active.

He'll make trades.

And at some point one of us will open Sleeper and see a transaction involving seven players and two picks without having any idea negotiations were happening.

### 10. #TrustTheProcess — JP

JP might have had one of the most important drafts in the league.

Lemon, Price and Mendoza gives him three legitimate swings at changing the direction of the roster.

The problem is that I think this is more interesting for 2027 than 2026.

The process may, in fact, be processing.

And after seven years, I'm still only about 90% sure his name is Josh.

We're too far in to ask now.

### 11. jordo41 — Jordan

Jordan gave us the most honest quote of the offseason during the scoring debate:

<PullQuote>
I'll do anything to win more games than I do, which isn't very many.
</PullQuote>

I don't know what analysis I can provide that improves upon that.

Baker Mayfield, David Montgomery and Garrett Wilson give him enough good players to beat somebody on any given week.

Turning that into a winning season has historically been the issue.

Regardless, he will talk like he's 11-1.

That's guaranteed.

### 12. King Henry Hill — Sam

This isn't really an insult.

Sam is rebuilding.

Carnell Tate and Kenyon Sadiq fit the plan, and Sam has been good enough historically that I'm not going to pretend he suddenly forgot how dynasty works.

He's just playing a different timeline than Deco or Jacob.

That doesn't mean he'll be harmless.

There is nothing more dangerous than playing Sam in Week 12 when he's 3-8, you're fighting for a playoff spot, and he has absolutely nothing to lose.

## My Preseason Playoff Six

If I have to pick them today:

**Deco. Jacob. Speers. Alex. Kyle. Hutch.**

Joel is the first team out, and I don't feel particularly confident about that.

Brandon is the team I could see making me look dumb fastest.

The middle of this league feels pretty wide open. From about fifth through ninth, one breakout player, one injury or one Matt trade could completely rearrange everything.

Which is exactly how it should be.

## Five Things I'm Calling Now

1. Deco leads the league in scoring but doesn't win the championship. Three straight finals is already ridiculous. Four would be absurd. Winning two of four would at least stop the Buffalo jokes.
2. Kyle makes the playoffs. I'm planting the flag. If this blows up, please quietly delete this issue from the archive.
3. Brandon finishes higher than eighth. There is too much young talent arriving at once for this roster to stay boring.
4. Hutch loses multiple games by less than five points. The fantasy gods keep receipts too.
5. At least one of Brandon, JP or Matt will look back at their three first-round picks and wonder what the hell happened. Nine picks between three people means somebody is going to look brilliant. Somebody else is going to own three cautionary tales.

## Final Thoughts

Here's the nice part.

Almost all of this will be wrong.

Somebody ranked ninth will start 3-0.

A top-four team will panic by Week 5.

Matt will make a trade.

Joel will make a poll.

Alex will respond eventually.

Jordan will talk shit.

Sam will ruin somebody's season.

Brandon will verbally accept something and then revisit the terms.

Hutch will win a game he had no business winning.

And Kyle will hear about Baker Mayfield whether Baker has anything to do with the conversation or not.

The scoring system is different.

The rosters are different.

The champion is different.

The league isn't.

Seven years of receipts behind us.

Time to make some new ones.

The 2026 Dynasty season is here.
