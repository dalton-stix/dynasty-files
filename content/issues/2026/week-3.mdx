The Supreme Commissioner has suffered a defeat.

By 1.01.

Kyle arrived at 0–2, having already played Alex and Deco, and beat Joel 140.49–139.48. His first three opponents are now a combined 8–1. The one loss belongs to him.

That is a miserable opening schedule and a hell of a way to finally get something back from it.

Joel scored enough to beat nine other teams. Unfortunately, our league continues to insist that you beat the specific person on your schedule. The portrait remains on the wall. The administration has lost its first vote.

Elsewhere, Jacob scored the most points and received Sleeper's worst-manager award. Brandon received its best-manager award and lost. Jordan won a football game without having to wait for the 2027 draft. Alex and Deco stayed undefeated.

Then Deco spent $56 on Ollie Gordon before most of the league had breakfast.

There is plenty to file.

## From the Chat: The Awards Committee Is Drunk

Fresh owner commentary was quiet in the chat I could read. Sleeper's automated Week 3 report handled the comedy itself.

**Best manager: Brandon. Result: lost by 13.76.**

**Worst manager: Jacob. Result: won by 68.71 and led the league in scoring.**

The labels measure how much of an optimized score you used. They do not measure whether the other guy spent Sunday getting run over by Jahmyr Gibbs.

Brandon used 118.89 of Sleeper's possible 124.50. Jacob used 147.20 of its possible 202.21. That larger ceiling includes Tyler Shough, who is on Jacob's current taxi squad. We don't have a historical taxi snapshot establishing that every hypothetical swap was available.

So congratulations to Brandon on his efficiency certificate. Congratulations to Jacob on the actual win.

One of those will be more useful in November.

## Kyle Has Entered the Palace

**kylelucas28 140.49, Amon Ra Doggin 139.48**

Brock Bowers delivered 26.10. James Cook added 23.32. Dak Prescott supplied 21.26, Deebo Samuel 15.56 and Houston's defense 16.00.

Kyle needed almost all of it.

Joel got 24.92 from Lamar Jackson, 21.74 from Kenneth Walker, 19.95 from Davante Adams and 19.82 from Javonte Williams. Nothing about 139.48 screams collapse. It was the third-best score in the league.

The problem was playing the second-best score.

There are maddening hindsight paths here. Trevor Lawrence's 27.10 instead of Lamar's 24.92 would have put Joel at 141.66. Matthew Golden scored 20.75 outside the lineup. That is arithmetic after the games, not proof that benching Lamar or Amon-Ra was an obvious decision before them.

Losing by 1.01 gives your bench the right to become irritating. It doesn't make every bench player a sensible pregame start.

Kyle moves to 1–2 with a win that says considerably more than the record. His opponents have been Alex, Deco and Joel. Getting through that stretch with one win keeps the playoff argument alive.

The man permanently memorialized for drafting Baker in the first round has now beaten the man permanently memorialized in a state portrait.

The Hall of Shame is developing a competitive division.

## Jacob's Terrible Management Continues

Again, those aren't extra standings wins. They're evidence that the repeat-champion discussion is becoming difficult to avoid.

Matt's best starter was Terry McLaurin at 18.20. Aaron Jones added 13.06 and Tony Pollard 12.96. Drake Maye's 7.08, Tetairoa McMillan's 3.20 and Hunter Henry's 1.25 left too much work for everybody else.

There was no secret bench rescue. Even an unrestricted all-player ceiling reaches only 104.58, well short of Jacob. Matt's roster could have held a very productive meeting and still lost by more than 40.

He's 1–2, eleventh in total scoring, and the new $7 tight end supplied 1.25. The procurement department will presumably remain open.
